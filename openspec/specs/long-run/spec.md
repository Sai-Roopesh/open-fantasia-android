# Long Run

## Purpose

The Story builds toward what the player came for without being dictated by an ending: the **Premise** it serves,
the **Arcs** it builds, the **Style** it is written in and the **Taste** it learns.

## Requirements

### Requirement: The Premise is what the Story serves

The Story SHALL keep returning to its **Premise** without fixating on it.

#### Scenario: The Premise keeps returning
- **GIVEN** the Premise is an enemies-to-lovers romance with Draconia
- **WHEN** ten Scenes are played
- **THEN** several of them move what is between Draconia and the Player Character, and the others are not about her

### Requirement: Arcs are built toward, never resolved on a schedule

Every Scene SHALL move at least one **Arc** a little, and no Arc SHALL be brought to an end because time is passing.
The player SHALL be able to add, rewrite or drop Arcs at any time.

#### Scenario: A slow burn stays slow
- **GIVEN** the Arc "Draconia comes round to Roopesh, slowly, against everything she was raised to be"
- **WHEN** twenty Scenes have passed
- **THEN** she has moved from insults toward slips and jealousy, and has not jumped ahead to declaring anything

#### Scenario: An Arc is dropped
- **GIVEN** the Arcs include "Harry and Roopesh become friends"
- **WHEN** the player drops it during Scene 15
- **THEN** the Scenes from then on on that Path no longer build toward it
- **AND** the Arcs at Scene 14, and on other Paths, still include it

### Requirement: The Director shapes the long run around Arcs and Landmarks

The **Director** SHALL shape the Story into **Stretches** that build, peak and rest around the **Arcs** and
**Landmarks**, each the next step of the current **Act** of the **Outline**. A Stretch is three to six days of story
time. Its plan SHALL say:
- what it builds toward, and its phase;
- the Scenes that matter in it, two to five, each with its day and time, its place, who is there and what it is for;
- the step each Arc may take in it;
- the move each **Lead** makes in it.

The Director SHALL know the Story's people and places, and SHALL name only the Story's people in a Scene that
matters; anyone else there is left unnamed. The Director SHALL know the **Engagements** ahead of the Player Character. A Scene that matters about something he has
arranged SHALL be that Engagement, at its time and place, and never a second meeting beside it.

The Director SHALL plan a new Stretch when the last one's days have passed or its Scenes have been played or passed
by, when a Scene has peaked, when a Landmark has passed, or when the player has gone off the plan: its next Scene that
matters passed without him. The Director SHALL know The Story So Far and the Scenes that mattered in the recent
Stretches, and SHALL plan none of the same shape again. It SHALL plan before the World moves on at a
Scene's end, so that what comes next follows the plan. The plan SHALL reach the Story only through the **Writer** and
the **Weaver**, and SHALL be visible only Backstage.

#### Scenario: The plan stays Backstage
- **WHEN** the Director plans the next Stretch
- **THEN** its plan appears in Backstage and nowhere in the Scene, the Day View, the World screen or the Journal

#### Scenario: A stretch that has not run its course
- **GIVEN** the Director planned a Stretch from Monday to Sunday, building toward the flying test on Saturday, with
  three Scenes that matter
- **WHEN** the first of them, on Tuesday, ends without peaking
- **THEN** the Director plans nothing new, and the Day View leads with the Stretch's next Scene

#### Scenario: A Scene that matters he arranged
- **GIVEN** he told Draconia to meet him in the library at ten on Saturday, and it was recorded
- **WHEN** the Director plans a Scene that matters about the debt with her
- **THEN** it is the library at ten on Saturday, and the Day View offers it once, as the Story's pick
- **AND** no second meeting with her about it is planned

#### Scenario: Only the Story's people
- **GIVEN** the Story has no librarian among its people
- **WHEN** the Director plans a Scene that matters in the library
- **THEN** everyone the Scene names is one of the Story's people, and the plan is not sent back for a name

#### Scenario: Off the plan
- **GIVEN** the Stretch's next Scene that matters is Draconia at breakfast on Monday
- **WHEN** the player skips to Tuesday instead
- **THEN** the Director plans again from where the Story now stands

#### Scenario: Not the same week twice
- **GIVEN** the last three Stretches each had Hermione at the library's end table
- **WHEN** the Director plans the next Stretch
- **THEN** no Scene that matters in it puts Hermione at the library's end table

### Requirement: Rest is deliberate

After a Scene that peaked, with a confrontation, a revelation or a change that cannot be undone, the Story's next pick
SHALL be quiet: a breath, of a day or so, before things move again. The **Director** SHALL judge the pace from the
recent Scenes as a whole, and a rest SHALL never take a whole **Stretch** by rule. A Scene
peaked when the **Writer** rated the Scene as a whole at that height; a quiet Scene is one where nothing is at stake, though the people
in it still want what they want.

#### Scenario: A quiet pick after a peak
- **GIVEN** the Scene where Draconia chose the Player Character in front of her House just ended
- **THEN** the Story's own pick at the Day View is a quiet Happening

#### Scenario: The rest the Director planned
- **GIVEN** a Scene just peaked, and the Director's new Stretch is a rest whose next Scene that matters is a quiet
  breakfast on Monday
- **THEN** the Story's own pick at the Day View is Monday's breakfast, and the World moves on at the first answer that
  offers it

#### Scenario: A breath, not a week
- **GIVEN** Scene 7 peaked
- **WHEN** the Director plans the Stretch after it
- **THEN** its first Scene that matters is quiet, and its others build again

### Requirement: Style is the player's own words

The **Style** SHALL be one piece of writing in the player's words, and everything that writes for the Story SHALL
read the same Style. Reply length (Short, Medium, Full) SHALL be set separately.

#### Scenario: Changing the Style mid-Story
- **WHEN** the player adds "keep the humour dry and British" to the Style
- **THEN** the Replies from then on are written that way

#### Scenario: Changing the reply length
- **GIVEN** the reply length is Full
- **WHEN** the player sets it to Short
- **THEN** the Replies from then on are short

### Requirement: Taste is short, readable and the player's

**Taste** SHALL hold explicit lasting preferences for each Story and for the player, without silently evicting
earlier instructions at a fixed line count. Shared preferences apply to every Story. A lesson SHALL go to the player's own only when it is about how they
like to read and not about one **World**. A Story's Taste SHALL belong to the Story, not to a **Path**: a **Redo** or
another Path SHALL never lose it. The player SHALL be able to read, edit and delete every line. Everything that writes
for the Story SHALL read both.

#### Scenario: Deleting a lesson
- **GIVEN** Taste has the line "keep Ron in the background"
- **WHEN** the player deletes it
- **THEN** later Scenes no longer keep Ron in the background on that account

#### Scenario: Rewording a lesson
- **GIVEN** Taste has the line "keep Ron in the background"
- **WHEN** the player rewords it to "keep Ron in the background unless he's needed"
- **THEN** later Scenes read the new words, and the old ones are gone from Taste

#### Scenario: A lesson outlives its Redo
- **GIVEN** the player redid a Reply with the Direction "never write her as a first-timer"
- **WHEN** the player plays on along the Reply that was written again
- **THEN** the original Direction remains active beside the Story and later Scenes receive it within its intended scope
- **AND** it is not replaced by an inferred Taste paraphrase

#### Scenario: The player's own Taste in a new Story
- **GIVEN** the player's own Taste says "don't decide what my character feels"
- **WHEN** they start The Night Train to Kalka for the first time
- **THEN** its Scenes keep to that line before anything is learned in it

### Requirement: Arcs keep pace with the run

The **Director** SHALL keep a roadmap of where each **Arc** should stand by each coming **Landmark**, from the whole
run of the Story. It SHALL revise the roadmap as the Story goes. Each Stretch's step for an Arc SHALL be as large as
reaching the next point on the roadmap needs, never as small as possible by rule, and never beyond what the Arc's
slow burn allows by that point.

#### Scenario: Four years of romance, paced
- **GIVEN** a Story whose romance Arc must run four years, with the Halloween troll eight weeks away
- **WHEN** the Director plans a Stretch in the first month
- **THEN** its roadmap says where Draconia should stand with him by Halloween, and the Stretch's step moves her
  toward it

#### Scenario: The roadmap follows the player
- **GIVEN** the roadmap expected a first public claim by Halloween
- **WHEN** the player's Moves bring it about in the second week
- **THEN** the next Stretch's roadmap moves its later points on from there, and does not hold the Arc back to wait
  for its dates

### Requirement: Leads act every Stretch

Every **Stretch** SHALL give each **Lead** a move about the **Player Character**: something they do, toward him, about
him or about someone near him. It is made in one of the Stretch's Scenes, or off-screen. A Lead SHALL not be put into
every Scene to make it, and a Stretch SHALL not give each Lead a Scene of their own by rule. A move made off-screen SHALL happen in the **World**, be recorded with who knows of it, and
reach him as **Meanwhile**, as something found, or as a consequence he meets.

#### Scenario: Jealousy acted on
- **GIVEN** Draconia saw him hold Hermione in the Great Hall
- **WHEN** the next Stretch is planned
- **THEN** Draconia's move in it acts on that, on screen or off: she claims him, or moves against Hermione

#### Scenario: A move made off-screen reaches him
- **GIVEN** a Stretch in which Draconia has no Scene with him
- **WHEN** its days pass
- **THEN** Backstage shows what she did about him that week, and he learns of it as someone would tell it or as
  something he finds

#### Scenario: Not one Scene per Lead
- **GIVEN** the Story has three Leads
- **WHEN** four Stretches are planned
- **THEN** in at least one of them a Lead's move is made off-screen, and a Scene that matters is about none of the
  Leads

### Requirement: The player steers with a brief and reactions

The player SHALL be able to say, at any time, what they want more of and less of in the Story, and react to any
**Scene** with "more like this" or "less like this". The **Director** SHALL plan every **Stretch** from the brief and
the recent reactions.

#### Scenario: Less library
- **GIVEN** the player reacted "less like this" to two library Scenes
- **WHEN** the Director plans the next Stretch
- **THEN** it puts no Scene that matters in the library

#### Scenario: A brief from the phone
- **WHEN** the player writes in the brief "more danger, more mysteries that aren't in the books"
- **THEN** the next Stretch brings a danger or a mystery of the Story's own
