# What Open Fantasia is for

To live inside other universes as one character: sometimes a small, straightforward story (a night on a train),
sometimes a grand one (years at Hogwarts). Every design decision is checked against these, in this order.

1. **Nothing that happened is forgotten.** Every person remembers what they lived through, and acts from who they are
   now, not who they were on the first day. Firsts and turning points stay with them for the whole story.
2. **The world is alive.** Things happen when the player is not there: people pursue what they want, plots move,
   consequences arrive. The player comes back to a world that went on without them.
3. **The emotions are real.** People want, fear, love and resent for reasons the story has earned, and the writing
   makes the player feel it.
4. **The story knows where it is going, all the way.** There is a direction for the whole story, roughly, not only
   for the next few days: where each relationship, mystery and arc is heading, made of everyone's own lives. After
   every Scene the story reconsiders that direction in the light of how the Scene went, and follows where the player
   takes it; within a Scene, the moment is improvised inside it.
5. **It is never boring.** Original plots, danger, mystery, everyday life, and something always moving.
6. **The player is their character, and only they are.** Nothing decides what the Player Character says, does,
   thinks or feels.
7. **A better story is worth waiting for, nothing else is.** The player will wait, before a Reply or at a Scene's end,
   for work that makes the story better: looking something up, thinking the plan through. Never for nothing, and
   always told what is happening.

The same design serves a small story and a grand one: a story's size changes how much there is to remember and plan,
not how it is done.
