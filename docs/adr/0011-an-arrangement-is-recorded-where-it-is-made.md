# An arrangement is recorded where it is made, and the Day View reads the records

An **Engagement** is something the Player Character knows is on at a time and place: a summons, an invitation, a
lesson, a session he agreed to. It is recorded once, when it is made, by the agent that saw it made:
- **The Chronicler**, from the Scene's page, quoting the Move or Reply it was made in word for word, as it quotes
  Moments. The same agent records it when a later Scene moves it or calls it off in front of him.
- **The Weaver**, when a line of Meanwhile carries one: a note under the door, a message passed on.

The Day View offers every Engagement still ahead, beside the Stretch's pick and the Weaver's own Happenings. Whether an
occurrence was kept or missed is decided in code from the Path. An expected one that passes without him goes on
without him, noticed, however the time passed, in a Gap or across a Scene.

The first play of The Malfoy Problem showed why. Five arrangements set up on the page were never offered. Among them
was McGonagall's Sunday summons, which then could not be noticed as missed. The reason was that offers were
re-derived by the Weaver after every Scene from the last twelve exchanges of prose, with nothing to check against.
Each part of the State keeps one writer (docs/adr/0004): the Chronicler already owns what happened on the page, and
an arrangement is something that happened there.

## Considered Options

- Telling the Weaver to offer what was set up: the failure itself, re-reading prose every time.
- The Writer declaring arrangements in its Beat Note: it plans before the Reply is written, and the Reply decides the
  words and the time.
- Making the Day View wait for the Chronicler: unnecessary. A record that lands later fills the Day View in, as the
  Day View already fills in part by part.
